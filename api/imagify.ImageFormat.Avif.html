AVIF format (.avif). AV1-based. Alpha support. Magic bytes: "ftyp" box with "avif" brand

AVIF has no switch for lossless encoding: it is a quality of AVIF_QUALITY_LOSSLESS out of AVIF_QUALITY_BEST, so the quality a write is asked for is what says whether the pixels are kept. The settings that have no other home are carried here instead: #speed, how long the encoder may take, and #alphaQuality, how hard the alpha plane is compressed. Both are honoured for a still image as well as for an animation.

Two more concern the colour the file holds rather than the effort spent on it: #subsampling, how finely the two colour-difference channels are stored, and #chromaDownsampling, the filter the conversion from RGB reduces them with. A setting is a value, or it is left unset, which is not the same thing: unset means the encoder keeps its own choice, and for the subsampling that choice differs between a still image and an animation.

speedInteger

How long the encoder may take, 0 being the slowest and most thorough and 10 the quickest. Unset means AvifConstants#DEFAULT_SPEED for a still image and AvifConstants#DEFAULT_ANIMATION_SPEED for an animation, which pays the cost of a speed setting once per frame.

alphaQualityInteger

How hard the alpha plane is compressed, AVIF_QUALITY_WORST being the worst and AvifConstants#QUALITY_BEST keeping every alpha value as it was given. Unset means AvifConstants#QUALITY_LOSSLESS, because lossy alpha is the single most visible AVIF artefact and an alpha plane is cheap to store.

subsamplingSubsampling

How finely the two colour-difference channels are stored, or null to leave the choice to the encoder, which is not one value but two: Subsampling#YUV444 for a still image, because a lone picture is where someone is most likely to be looking closely at a saturated edge, and Subsampling#YUV420 for an animation, where the cost of the alternative is paid once per frame.

Naming a value here overrides both, which is the point of the setting: a caller who wants the smallest animation there is asks for Subsampling#YUV420 on a still image too, and a caller who wants a text screenshot as an animation asks for Subsampling#YUV444 there as well.

chromaDownsamplingChromaDownsampling

The filter the conversion from RGB reduces the colour-difference channels with, or null to leave the choice to the encoder, which is ChromaDownsampling#AUTOMATIC.

This is a second, narrower thing than #subsampling: it decides how the reduction is done, not whether it happens, and it therefore has nothing at all to do with a file stored as Subsampling#YUV444, where no channel is ever reduced. On a Subsampling#YUV420 file it is worth having, because reducing by averaging keeps colour edges from bleeding into one another while ChromaDownsampling#SHARP_YUV keeps them from smearing, and the two are not the same picture.
